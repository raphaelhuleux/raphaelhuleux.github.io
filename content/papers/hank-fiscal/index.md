##### Abstract

We study the role of expectations regarding aggregate risk in two standard HANK models, using a novel deep learning approach that approximates their solutions in moving-average form. In a simple Intertemporal Keynesian Cross model, we find strong sign, size and state dependence in the impulse responses to deficit-financed transfer shocks. These nonlinearities are, however, already present in the nonlinear perfect-foresight solution. Aggregate risk slightly lowers the MPC, as fewer households are hand-to-mouth. Households also stay further from the borrowing constraint, which dampens the response to negative fiscal shocks. In a larger HANK model, the nonlinearities are weaker, and the dynamics under aggregate risk and under perfect foresight again coincide.
